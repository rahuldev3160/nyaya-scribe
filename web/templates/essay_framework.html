Essay Framework Guide

The A-B-C formula for 125-mark UPSC essays
Part A — Introduction · 150–180 words
A1 Hook
Open with a quote, data point, or historical fact that frames the essay topic and grabs attention.
A2 Context
2–3 sentences situating the topic — historical background, current relevance, and why it matters now.
A3 Thesis
Your central argument in one clear sentence. This is what the examiner will score against.
A4 Signpost
Tell the reader what dimensions you will cover — sets up the body structure and shows planning.
Part B — Body · 650–750 words
Dimension Block Micro-Moves
Claim
State your position on this dimension.
Evidence
Data, case study, scheme, or fact that proves the claim.
Analysis
Explain WHY and HOW — connect evidence to thesis.
Counter
Acknowledge the strongest opposing argument.
Rebuttal
Refute the counter while absorbing what is valid.
Mandatory Blocks
Challenges
What stands in the way — structural, political, or social.
Solutions
Concrete, policy-linked steps forward.
Synthesis Para (optional)
Bridge all dimensions back to the thesis before conclusion.
Part C — Conclusion · 120–150 words
C1 Synthesis
Restate your thesis in light of the body — show that you proved it.
C2 Way Forward
2–3 actionable steps — policy, institutional, or societal.
C3 Philosophical
Zoom out — connect to a larger value, constitutional principle, or civilisational idea.
C4 Closing Line
One memorable sentence — quote, aphorism, or forward-looking call that leaves an impression.
Body Frameworks — which to use when
Framework Best For
PESTLE Policy, governance, economic reform, infrastructure topics
SPIDER Social issues, rights, gender, education, health
IDEA Technology, innovation, future-focused, disruption themes
PPF Past-Present-Future narrative essays, civilisational arcs
INDIVIDUAL–SOCIETY Ethics-adjacent, values, rights vs. duties, freedom essays
CUSTOM When no standard lens fits — build your own dimension list in A4 Signpost
Hook Types — A1 reference
Type How to use it
QUOTE Cite a thinker, statesman, or document. Must be directly relevant — don't force it.
DATA A statistic that reveals the scale of the issue. Source matters (government report, global index).
HISTORICAL FACT A turning point in history that parallels the essay topic — connects past to present.
CONTEMPORARY A recent event, policy, or development (last 1–2 years) that makes the topic urgent.
LITERARY REF An allusion to literature, mythology, or philosophy. Shows breadth. Use sparingly.
RHETORICAL Q A question you will answer through the essay. Engages the reader. Works best for abstract topics.
